# Fitness Equipment Brand --- Prototype Website Build Plan

> **Goal:** Build a high-fidelity prototype that is credible enough to
> show local gym-equipment manufacturers and explain how the brand will
> market and sell their products.
>
> **This is NOT the final e-commerce store.** Products, prices,
> specifications, manufacturer relationships, delivery promises,
> warranties, and imagery remain placeholders until supplier agreements
> are confirmed.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Core Brand Idea

### Internal positioning

**We make/select equipment for people who train, not people who merely
work out.**

This is the idea behind the site. It does not have to be the final
public tagline.

### Customer transformation

**B2C customer**

> "I work out." → **"I train seriously."**

**B2B gym owner**

> "I own a room with equipment." → **"I built somewhere serious people
> want to train."**

### Marketing framework

Use this order throughout the website:

**IDENTITY → OUTCOME → PROOF → PRODUCT → PURCHASE**

Example:

-   **Identity:** I am someone who trains seriously.
-   **Outcome:** I want equipment that helps me progress.
-   **Proof:** Materials, construction, specifications, testing,
    warranty.
-   **Product:** The actual rack, dumbbell, gripper, plate, etc.
-   **Purchase:** Buy, join waitlist, or request a quote.

# 11. Visual System

## Direction

Think:

**Industrial + precise + restrained + serious.**

Visual themes:

-   Steel
-   Rubber
-   Chalk
-   Knurling
-   Welds
-   Plates
-   Bars
-   Shadows
-   Training environments
-   Close-up product details

## Layout

Use:

-   Large typography
-   Large product imagery
-   Generous whitespace
-   Few words
-   Strong hierarchy
-   Long scroll sections
-   Subtle motion

Avoid:

-   Neon green everywhere
-   Flames
-   "BEAST MODE"
-   Excessive gradients
-   Five different fonts
-   Countdown timers
-   Fake discounts
-   Fake scarcity
-   Fake reviews
-   Fake customers
-   "India's #1"

-   "Premium quality" without proof

# 17. Motion

Keep animation restrained.

Good:

-   Gentle image reveal
-   Fade/translate on scroll
-   Product image scale of \~1--3%
-   Smooth section transitions
-   Subtle hover states

Bad:

-   Constant movement
-   3D spinning everything
-   Excessive parallax
-   Loading animations
-   Cursor gimmicks
-   Animation that slows navigation

The product should feel important, not the animation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 18. What to Show Manufacturers

During a meeting:

1.  Open the homepage.
2.  Explain the positioning in one sentence.
3.  Scroll through the curated categories.
4.  Open the flagship product.
5.  Explain that imagery/specifications are placeholders.
6.  Show how their actual product could eventually be presented.
7.  Open **For Gyms**.
8.  Show the quotation flow.
9.  Explain that the site is intentionally pre-launch.
10. Discuss the commercial arrangement.

Suggested explanation:

> "This is a private pre-launch prototype. I deliberately haven't listed
> any manufacturer's actual products yet because I'm finalizing
> manufacturing relationships first. My plan is to select a small number
> of strong products, create high-quality product presentation and
> content around them, handle customer acquisition and sales, and work
> with a manufacturing partner for fulfilment. I'd like to understand
> your dealer pricing and whether confirmed orders can be dispatched
> directly to customers."

# 19. Questions for Manufacturers

Bring these to every supplier conversation.

### Commercial

-   What is your dealer/reseller pricing?
-   Is there an MOQ?
-   Is there a minimum monthly purchase?
-   Are prices inclusive or exclusive of GST?
-   Can pricing improve with volume?

### Fulfilment

-   Can you dispatch directly to my customer?
-   Can orders be sent without showing your retail price?
-   Whose invoice goes inside the shipment?
-   Can packaging eventually use my brand?
-   Which transport companies do you use?
-   How is freight calculated?

### Products

-   Which products sell best through dealers?
-   Which products have the strongest dealer margins?
-   Which products are difficult/expensive to ship?
-   Which products have high return or damage rates?
-   Can I use your product photography and specifications?
-   Can I photograph products myself?

### Service

-   Who handles warranty claims?
-   Who handles transit damage?
-   Who handles installation?
-   Can you provide service outside Gujarat?
-   How are replacement parts handled?

### Brand Development

-   Do you manufacture private-label equipment?
-   What MOQ is required for custom branding?
-   Can colours/materials/dimensions be customized?
-   Can products eventually be manufactured to my specifications?
-   Can packaging be customized?

# 21. Definition of Done

The prototype is finished when you can hand your phone to someone and
they understand within roughly one minute:

1.  This is a fitness-equipment brand.
2.  It is focused on serious training.
3.  It sells to individuals.
4.  It also supplies/equips gyms.
5.  Products are presented differently from a generic wholesale
    catalogue.
6.  Gym owners can request a quote.
7.  The company is pre-launch.
8.  Manufacturer products have not been falsely represented as yours.

At that point:

**STOP CODING.**

Go talk to manufacturers.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 22. What Happens After Manufacturer Meetings

Once a supplier agrees to work with you:

1.  Obtain written permission to market their products.
2.  Obtain dealer price list.
3.  Confirm MOQ.
4.  Confirm direct-dispatch process.
5.  Confirm invoice arrangement.
6.  Confirm freight.
7.  Confirm warranty.
8.  Confirm installation.
9.  Confirm returns/damage procedure.
10. Obtain approved specifications.
11. Obtain approved product imagery or photograph samples yourself.
12. Calculate gross margin.
13. Calculate maximum affordable customer-acquisition cost.
14. Replace placeholder products with real products.
15. Order samples of the products you intend to advertise.
16. Create original photos/video.
17. Implement checkout only for products suited to direct online
    purchase.
18. Keep expensive commercial projects on a quotation flow.
19. Run small advertising tests.
20. Scale only after unit economics work.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Final Principle

The prototype is **not supposed to convince manufacturers that you are
already a huge retailer**.

It should convince them that:

> **You have a clear idea of who you want to sell to, how you intend to
> present the products, and how you can potentially create demand for
> their manufacturing capacity.**

Build enough to demonstrate that.

Then get out of the editor and start having supplier conversations.
